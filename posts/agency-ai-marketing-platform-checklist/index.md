To evaluate an AI marketing platform for your agency, test one client workflow end to end: context, creative, approval, account and budget, assistant handoff, reporting, and the cost of adding clients. A useful platform has to work beyond a polished demo. The real test is
whether you can take a client from a clear brief to reviewed creative, a deliberate
launch decision, and a report that explains what happened.

Start with one client and one goal. Before migrating your portfolio, walk through
these seven checks with the same brief you would use in your day-to-day work.

Replacing an agency rather than choosing software? That handover is a different job. Start with our [agency transition checklist](/blog/switching-ad-agencies-transition-checklist) for account ownership, exports and the cutover date, then come back here to evaluate tools.

## 1. Keep the client context clear

Bring the client's website, offer, target audience, brand assets and approval
contact. Confirm which workspace is selected before asking an assistant to draft
copy or summarize results. A saved brand voice is useful only when the right
client's instructions are used.

Try this prompt: “Summarize the selected client's offer, audience and brand voice.
List anything you still need from me before preparing creative.”

## 2. Review an actual creative draft

Test a real deliverable, not just a strategy response. Review the headline, image
or video, call to action, destination URL and intended channel. Check that any
product details and numerical claims match the material you supplied.

Ask for a revision and confirm it appears in the final draft. This catches a
common handoff problem: feedback is acknowledged in conversation but never makes
it into the asset that someone eventually launches.

## 3. Identify the approval moment

Agree on who approves creative and who authorizes publication or advertising
spend. A draft, a scheduled post and a launched campaign are different states.
Make those states visible to everyone who works on the account.

Try this instruction: “Prepare the draft and show me the final copy, asset and
destination account. Wait for my approval before publishing.”

## 4. Check the account and budget together

Before launch, review the selected client, advertising account, destination,
campaign dates and budget. Keep the software subscription and advertising spend
separate in your own cost calculation. If you serve several clients, record which
client owns each cost rather than reporting only an agency-wide total.

## 5. Test the assistant handoff

If you plan to use ChatGPT, Claude or another assistant, test the exact workflow
you intend to repeat. Can the assistant retrieve the selected client's context,
prepare the requested work, and explain what it changed? A connector listing by
itself is not a completed workflow.

Adessa's [MCP guide](/api-access) includes example prompts for strategy, social
content drafts and client reporting. Use the guided setup to connect the assistant
you intend to use, then try one small task from beginning to end.

## 6. Read a report with consistent definitions

Compare the same date range and timezone. Identify which platform supplied each
metric and how conversions were counted. Missing data should stay marked as
missing; a blank result is not automatically zero sales.

For a small first test, prioritize whether the report is understandable and
traceable. Do not call a creative a winner after a handful of clicks. Record spend,
results and any attribution gaps before deciding what to change next.

## 7. Calculate the cost of adding clients

Check the base subscription, included workspaces, additional-client pricing and
any usage limits that matter to your workload. Test the workflow with two clients
before extrapolating to twenty.

## Before you commit: test the exit

The seven checks show whether a platform can run your client work. One more test shows whether you can leave cleanly when a client moves on or your agency changes tools. Run it during the trial, while switching is still cheap.

- **Reports:** Export one client report in the format you would hand over, such as PDF or CSV. Check that it includes the date range, spend and data source, not only charts.
- **Creative:** Download a finished asset with its final copy. Confirm you receive the revised version, not an earlier draft.
- **Ad accounts:** List which ad accounts the client owns and which campaigns run through the platform. For client-owned accounts, note who has admin access and how to remove the platform's access. For campaigns the platform launches, ask what happens to running campaigns and their history if the client leaves.
- **Assistant access:** If you connected ChatGPT, Claude or another assistant, find where to disconnect it or revoke its key. Then confirm it can no longer read that client's information.
- **Billing:** Find out how to remove a client workspace and when its billing stops.

## A simple acceptance sheet

Copy this into a shared document and fill in the observed result—not just a tick beside the feature name.

| Check | Test to run | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Client context | Switch between two clients and request each brief | Correct client name and approved facts in each response |
| Creative revision | Change one headline, then reopen the saved draft | The revised headline in the actual asset |
| Approval | Prepare a draft without authorizing publication | A saved draft and no published post |
| Account and budget | Review a campaign before launch | Client, account, destination, dates and budget together |
| Assistant handoff | Complete one supported task in your chosen assistant | The saved result and its status, not only a chat response |
| Reporting | Compare the same dates across two client reports | Data source, timezone, conversion definition and missing data |
| Client costs | Calculate the bill at two client counts | Subscription, additional workspaces and ad spend separated |
| Exit | Export one report and one asset, then list each account's owner | A file you could hand over, plus how to remove access and stop billing |

For every row, add an owner, expected result, observed result, evidence link and remaining question. A missing feature can be a deliberate tradeoff; an unknown result means the test is not finished.

### Example: a revision that should stay a draft

Here is an illustrative test, not a customer result. A gym asks you to change a trial-class caption from Tuesday to Thursday. Save the revision, reopen the draft and confirm the date changed. Then check the destination account and verify that nothing was published merely because the revision was saved. If your process cannot distinguish those steps, fix the handoff before adding more clients.
